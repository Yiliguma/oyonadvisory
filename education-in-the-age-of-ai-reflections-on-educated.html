What Does Education Mean in the Age of AI? Reflections on Educated

August 8, 2026

If AI allows us to learn almost anything from anywhere, what will education really mean in the future?

By Yili · Revised by ChatGPT

Share on LinkedIn

I recently finished Educated by Tara Westover. I grew up in a completely different country, culture, and family from Tara, yet I felt deeply connected to her story—especially her struggle to become herself and claim ownership of her own life through education.

Perhaps many of us eventually reach a point when we begin to reflect on our upbringing and question some of what we were taught. This often happens when we encounter a world very different from the one we came from and realize that what once seemed unquestionably true may have been only one version of reality.

But recognizing that difference is only the first step. Then comes the harder work: understanding why we were raised the way we were, deciding what we still believe and what we no longer do, and learning to live with different—and sometimes conflicting—ways of seeing the world.

That process can bring uncertainty and even guilt. Questioning inherited beliefs can feel like questioning the people and communities we came from. Yet once we have encountered other ways of seeing the world, simply returning to the old one may no longer be possible.

This made me think about education in the age of AI. AI can make acquiring knowledge far more efficient and accessible. Perhaps, in the future, fewer people will need to leave their hometown, community, or even culture simply to gain access to knowledge available somewhere else.

But I wonder: if Tara had been able to learn everything from home, without physically entering radically different communities and ways of life, would she have become the same Tara?

Perhaps education is not only about gaining knowledge. At its deepest level, it may also be about becoming better able to receive different ideas, cultures, people, and ways of living—without accepting or rejecting them automatically.

Education gives us more worlds to see. Maturity may lie in learning how to understand them, live with their contradictions, and ultimately make our own choices.
