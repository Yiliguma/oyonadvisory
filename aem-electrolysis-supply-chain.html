AEM Electrolysis — From Better Membranes to Industrial Systems

November 13, 2026

Anion exchange membrane (AEM) electrolysis is moving from small commercial units into industrial demonstrations. Its promise is a compact electrolyzer that can use lower-cost catalyst materials. The commercial question is whether those savings survive years of operation, stack replacements and the cost of running the full plant. [1] [4] [5]

Share on LinkedIn

What is changing in the technology?

AEM electrolyzers use a solid polymer membrane to carry hydroxide ions between the electrodes. The alkaline environment allows developers to use catalysts based on more abundant metals, such as nickel and iron. The membrane and the ionomer, which provides an ion-conducting path within the catalyst layer, must maintain conductivity and mechanical integrity through prolonged operation. That makes the membrane, ionomer and electrode a system to qualify together. [1] [2] [3]

Two development paths are taking shape. One uses dilute potassium hydroxide to improve ion transport and cell performance. The other pursues pure-water operation, which could simplify electrolyte handling but makes local ion transport and catalyst-layer stability harder to manage. A 2026 study reported roughly 1,400 hours at 1 A/cm² in a pure-water-fed research cell by engineering the local alkaline environment. The next step is to show how that approach holds up in larger stacks under variable load. [2]

Where commercialization stands

  • Small commercial systems. Germany’s Enapter supplied modular units for a Tokyo Gas station that began producing and selling hydrogen in 2023. [4] [18]
  • Industrial operation. US-based Power to Hydrogen reported starting hydrogen production from a 0.5 MW system at Antwerp-Bruges in September 2026. Japan’s Asahi Kasei announced in November that the companies would evaluate manufacturing scale-up and commercialization. [5] [6] [21]
  • Larger pilots and orders. US-based EVOLOH announced a 2.5 MW pilot agreement at a 3M facility, with operation planned for 2027 subject to approvals. In China, Shanghai Qingluan reportedly raised more than RMB 100 million in Series A financing and won awards for MW-class demonstrations. Public operating data for those projects remain limited. [7] [8]

Japan’s role in the supply chain

Japanese industry is working across membranes, electrodes and equipment. NEDO project records identify Tokuyama, Nippon Shokubai and Nippon Kayaku in membrane or ionomer development. TOPPAN, De Nora Permelec, Noritake and SCREEN are working on electrode coating, transport layers, catalysts or membrane–electrode assemblies. Fuji Electric is developing AEM cells and equipment, and Sumitomo Electric is pursuing a long-duration field demonstration alongside its work on porous nickel transport layers. [11] [12] [13] [14] [15] [16] [17]

What to watch next

For materials suppliers such as Ionomr and Versogen, the milestone is repeat qualification across multiple stack manufacturers. For system developers, it is repeat industrial orders backed by disclosed plant-level electricity use, availability and stack replacement intervals. Those figures will show whether AEM’s materials advantage translates into a lower cost per kilogram of hydrogen. [1] [9] [10]
